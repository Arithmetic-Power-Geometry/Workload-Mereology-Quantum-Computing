# Prior-Art Boundary

Workload–mereology quantum computing lies at the intersection of several established research directions. The following neighboring ideas predate this framework:

- generalized tensor-product structures and minimal-scrambling subsystem selection;
- mereological quantum phase transitions;
- quantum-error-correction code switching and switching-cost optimization;
- workload-aware qubit partitioning, placement, routing, and scheduling;
- adaptive and reconfigurable quantum hardware;
- generic optimization with service or task costs and switching costs.

## Distinguishing scientific question

The framework studies a different combined optimization problem: quantum subsystem structure itself is treated as a variational choice under direct competition between computational task locality and dynamical naturalness.

For the dimensionless formulation,

[
J_\lambda(F)=\widehat S_{\mathrm{OQM}}(F)+\lambda\widehat C_T(F),
]

the physical term is derived from the OQM Gaussian scrambling criterion and the computational term measures workload interactions cut by the candidate bipartition. The resulting optimum can coincide with the physical endpoint, the task endpoint, or a strict intermediate factorization.

The lower-convex-envelope characterization identifies the factorizations that can become uniquely optimal as workload pressure varies. A strict intermediate lower-hull vertex produces a nonempty interval in which a genuine compromise factorization is preferred.

The contribution therefore concerns the explicit **workload–mereology competition** and its analytical and computational consequences. It does not claim novelty for generalized tensor-product structures, scrambling-based subsystem selection, quantum code switching, workload-aware qubit routing, or adaptive hardware individually.

No claim is made of new quantum mechanics, computation beyond BQP, quantum supremacy, or demonstrated quantum speedup.
